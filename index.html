Dual-Parameter Lubricant Health Assessment

Unlike modern dashboard counters that guess oil wear based purely on odometer intervals, this mechanical system evaluates physical fluid mechanics: how thick it flows and how much light passes through its carbon suspension.

1. Kinematic Consistency (cSt)

Thermal shearing in crankpin journals reduces polymer chain lengths, causing thinning, while severe high-temperature oxidation causes polymerization and heavy sludge. We compare current cSt against factory SAE baseline.

Shear Ratio: R_v = ν_current / ν_nominal

2. Optical Transparency (%)

Sub-micron carbon soot, nitration byproducts, and metallic blow-by absorb incident photons. Optical transmittance decays following a modified Beer-Lambert exponential function, indicating particulate contamination.

Beer-Lambert Decay: T = T_0 · e^(-α · C_soot)

3. Remaining Useful Life (RUL)

Our formula synthesizes the consistency degradation factor and soot loading with your engine duty cycle (highway vs stop & go) to predict remaining safe kilometers before hydrodynamic boundary friction occurs.

RUL_km = D_max · M_duty · [(OHI - 25) / 75]

The 5-Stage Oil Degradation Spectrum

Quick visual comparison reference for automotive mechanics inspecting dipsticks and oil filter cartridges.

Fresh Induction

85% - 100% Transmittance

Golden amber translucent. Full additive package reserve, zero soot suspension, optimal ZDDP wear protection.

Mid-Life Stable

60% - 84% Transmittance

Rich caramel brown. Normal thermal coloring; detergent additives actively suspending fine particles.

Degraded Amber

35% - 59% Transmittance

Dark roasted brown. Antioxidants partially depleted; viscosity begins shifting due to thermal shear or light thickening.

Soot Saturated

15% - 34% Transmittance

Deep black with opaque edges. Common in diesel engines; dispersants reaching saturation point. Change due soon.

Critical Sludge

< 15% Transmittance

Pitch black, tarry consistency. Severe risk of ring sticking, filter bypass opening, and journal bearing starvation.

Empirical Formulation

Pure Mathematical Models

No black boxes. The calculations implemented in the diagnostic bench are strictly derived from tribological mechanics and fluid degradation laws.

1. Viscosity Consistency Score (S_visc)

Evaluates deviation of Kinematic Viscosity (ν) at 100°C from nominal SAE factory grade. Both excessive thinning (fuel dilution/shear) and thickening (sludge) are penalized exponentially:

S_visc = 100 × exp(-2.8 × |( ν_curr / ν_0 ) - 1.0|^1.5)

Where ν_curr is current measured viscosity and ν_0 is nominal grade centerpoint (e.g. 10.5 cSt for SAE 5W-30).

2. Optical Transmittance Score (S_trans)

Translates percentage light transmittance (T%) measured across a 1mm fluid gap into a normalized optical health factor:

S_trans = ( T / 100 )^0.75 × 100

The 0.75 exponent models human visual discernment and non-linear soot particle opacity clustering.

3. Composite Oil Health Index (OHI)

A multi-parameter weighted score combining optical soot loading (55%) and hydrodynamic shear consistency (45%):

OHI = 0.55 × S_trans + 0.45 × S_visc

If OHI drops below 30%, immediate lubricant change is mandatory to prevent metal-on-metal hydrodynamic breakdown.

4. Duty-Cycle Remaining Useful Life (RUL)

Calculates remaining safe operating distance before reaching the critical wear threshold:

RUL_km = max( 0 , D_nominal × M_duty × [(OHI - 25) / 75] )

Where M_duty is the product of Base Oil Factor, Driving Mode Factor, and Engine Type Factor.
